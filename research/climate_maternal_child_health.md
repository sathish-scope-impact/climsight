# Climate Change and Maternal, Newborn, Child Health (MNCH)

## Research Summary

Last updated: 2026-03-05

This document synthesizes the current evidence base on how climate change affects maternal, newborn, and child health outcomes, with focus on heat exposure, and what adaptation interventions have been tested.

---

## 1. Heat Exposure and Maternal Health

### Preterm Birth

- Each 1C increase in ambient temperature is associated with ~5% increase in preterm birth likelihood ([PLOS Climate 2025](https://journals.plos.org/climate/article/file?id=10.1371/journal.pclm.0000637&type=printable))
- Heatwave days associated with ~16% increase in preterm birth risk ([PLOS Climate 2025](https://journals.plos.org/climate/article/file?id=10.1371/journal.pclm.0000637&type=printable))
- Projected impact (India, 2025-2035): 353,000 heat-related preterm births across urban India
- Heat-health warning systems could avert ~6,350 preterm births in Indian cities

### Stillbirth

- CHAMNHA project found extreme heat associated with increased stillbirth rates in Kenya and Burkina Faso ([CHAMNHA/LSHTM](https://www.lshtm.ac.uk/research/centres-projects-groups/chamnha))
- Dose-response relationship identified: risk increases with duration of heat exposure, not just peak temperature

### Maternal Hypertension

- Heat exposure during pregnancy linked to increased gestational hypertension
- Mechanism: heat stress impairs thermoregulation, increases cardiovascular strain
- Compounded by dehydration in water-scarce settings

### Neonatal Outcomes

- Neonatal dehydration risk increases during heat events
- Breastfeeding disruption: heat stress reduces milk production and feeding frequency
- Skin rashes and heat-related illness in newborns
- Reduced health facility access during extreme heat (women avoid travel)

### Key Reference

> "Climate change represents a fundamental threat to human health, with pregnant women and newborns being more susceptible than other populations." -- [WHO Overview of Reviews, JOGH 2024](https://jogh.org/2024/jogh-14-04128)

## 2. CHAMNHA Project -- The Most Relevant Direct Evidence

**Full name:** Climate, Heat and Maternal and Neonatal Health in Africa

**Consortium:** LSHTM (lead), Aga Khan University (Kenya), IRSS (Burkina Faso), Wits RHI (South Africa), Umea University (Sweden), Norwegian Research Council

**Duration:** 3 years (funded by Belmont Forum)

**Significance:** Largest heat x MNH intervention study in sub-Saharan Africa

### Findings

#### Kenya (Kilifi County)

- Extreme hot weather associated with adverse outcomes across: stillbirth, premature birth, skin rashes, breastfeeding disruption, neonatal dehydration, maternal wellbeing, maternal hypertension, health facility access
- **Critical insight:** "For women in Kenya, heat exposure and water shortages are inextricably linked -- interventions addressing either hazard in isolation may have limited efficacy"
- Community health volunteers can effectively monitor heat stress and disseminate alerts to pregnant/postpartum women
- Co-designed cooler community spaces showed promise

#### Burkina Faso

- Women reported heat-related symptoms in themselves and their fetus
- Extreme heat creates obstacles to health facility access and care quality
- Behavior change interventions (interviews, videos, illustrated discussions) reduced adverse health impacts
- Integration of heat-health activities into routine practice is feasible

#### Intervention Results (2024-2025)

- Women reported better understanding of heat effects on their health and newborn health
- Increased knowledge of importance of staying hydrated, frequent breastfeeding, avoiding heavy clothing for newborns
- Mothers-in-law and male spouses began assisting with household chores during heat events
- Community health influencers can be trained to include heat-health messaging in daily routines

### Publications

- Kenya community intervention: [Health Policy & Planning 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/heapol/czaf028)
- Burkina Faso intervention integration: [Health Policy & Planning 2025](https://academic.oup.com/heapol/article-pdf/40/7/708/63386252/czaf030.pdf)
- Ethnographic study (Burkina Faso): BMJ Global Health, February 2024
- CHAMNHA project page: [LSHTM](https://www.lshtm.ac.uk/research/centres-projects-groups/chamnha)

## 3. Evidence Gap Maps

### Climate x Maternal Health (PLOS Global Public Health, 2024)

- 133 studies integrated into evidence gap map
- Africa and Caribbean had fewer studies -- research attention disparity
- Extreme heat emerged as most studied factor
- Gaps: drought, malnutrition, food insecurity impacts on maternal health
- Source: [PLOS 2024](https://journals.plos.org/globalpublichealth/article?id=10.1371/journal.pgph.0003540)

### Climate x Child Health (PMC, 2024)

- Evidence gap map covering impact of climate change on child health outcomes
- Identifies knowledge gaps to establish foundation for evidence-based interventions
- Source: [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11474898/)

### MNCH in Heat Health Action Plans (PMC, 2025)

- 83% of Heat Health Action Plans (HHAPs) identified children as at-risk population ([PMC 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12143117/))
- 52% named pregnant women as at-risk ([PMC 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12143117/))
- Only 39% specified newborns/infants separately
- Only 14% mentioned postpartum or breastfeeding mothers
- **Only 4 plans** included interventions to improve care in maternity/neonatal facilities
- **No plans analyzed outcomes by pregnancy status**

## 4. WHO and Global Policy Developments

### PMNCH Dialogue (February 2025)

- 300+ global health and climate leaders participated ([PMNCH 2025](https://pmnch.who.int/news-and-events/news/item/03-03-2025-addressing-the-impacts-of-climate-change-on-maternal-newborn-and-child-health-and-building-climate-resilient-societies))
- Focused on unique impacts of climate change on pregnant/lactating women, newborns, children, adolescents
- Referenced scoping study on "How does climate change impact women and children across agroecological zones in India"
- Key interventions discussed for climate mitigation and adaptation

### WHO Projections

- 250,000 climate-related deaths annually by 2050 ([WHO estimate](https://www.ssph-journal.org/journals/public-health-reviews/articles/10.3389/phrs.2024.1607553/full))
- Disproportionately affecting maternal and child health
- Need for adaptive healthcare models addressing malnutrition, infectious diseases, heat stress in pregnant women and children ([PMC 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11738619/))
- Clear interventions to address climate change impacts on MNCH "rarely proposed or elaborated" in national policies ([JOGH 2024](https://jogh.org/2024/jogh-14-04128))

### Integrating MCH into Climate Change (Public Health Reviews, 2024-2025)

- Future research should focus on vulnerable populations in low-resource settings
- Cost-effective interventions aimed at reducing maternal and neonatal mortality during climate-related disasters are critical
- Need to evaluate long-term consequences of prenatal exposure to climate-related pollutants
- Sources: [SSPH+](https://www.ssph-journal.org/journals/public-health-reviews/articles/10.3389/phrs.2024.1607553/full), [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11738619/)

## 5. Climate Education as Adaptation

### Scoping Review (ScienceDirect, 2025)

- Reviewed climate education programs focused on maternal and newborn health
- Most programs implemented in LMICs and United States
- Aimed to improve healthcare provider and/or patient understanding of climate-specific risks to MNH
- 4 programs conducted formal evaluations -- all reported positive outcomes
- 2 programs that assessed health impacts found **significant reductions in adverse climate-related health outcomes** following MNH-specific climate education
- Source: [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2667278225001087)

## 6. Key Gaps Identified Across Literature

1. **Intervention specificity:** Current adaptation strategies are largely nonspecific to the unique vulnerabilities of pregnant women and young children
2. **Geographic coverage:** Evidence heavily weighted toward high-income countries; notable dearth of literature from Global South
3. **Cost-effectiveness data:** Panelists consistently flag "lack of knowledge on cost-effective and affordable solutions for LMICs"
4. **Monitoring:** Heat action plans don't track MNCH outcomes; no system monitors whether climate-health plans for pregnant women are implemented or effective
5. **Integrated approaches:** Heat and water scarcity interventions treated separately, but they are inextricably linked for pregnant women in many settings
6. **Long-term developmental impacts:** Prenatal climate exposure effects on child development largely unstudied

---

## 7. Implications for CHART

- **The evidence supports building CHART** -- climate impacts on MNCH are documented, interventions exist but are scattered, and no tool currently operationalizes this knowledge
- **Start with heat** -- strongest evidence base, most actionable interventions, clear seasonal patterns
- **Kenya first** -- CHAMNHA provides Kenya-specific intervention evidence; national framework ready
- **Intervention KB will be incomplete** -- must be designed for iterative expansion as evidence grows
- **Monitor what others don't** -- tracking MNCH outcomes against climate triggers would be genuinely novel
