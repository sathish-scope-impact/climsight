# Country Context: Kenya and India

## Research Summary

Last updated: 2026-03-05

This document provides the country-specific context for deploying CHART in Kenya (recommended first pilot) and India (second phase), covering health system structure, climate-health landscape, policy frameworks, and existing digital infrastructure.

---

## 1. Kenya

### Why Kenya First

1. **National framework ready:** [RMNCAH-N 2025-2030](https://countdown2030.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/RMNXAH-N-for-Official-Processing.pdf) explicitly includes climate change interventions
2. **Direct evidence available:** [CHAMNHA](https://www.lshtm.ac.uk/research/centres-projects-groups/chamnha) produced Kenya-specific intervention evidence ([Kilifi county](https://doi.org/10.1093/heapol/czaf028))
3. **Well-structured health system:** 47 counties with County Health Management Teams
4. **DHIS2 operational:** KHIS (Kenya Health Information System) covers all counties since 2011
5. **Active climate-health research community:** Aga Khan University, KEMRI, LSHTM partnerships
6. **Seasonal forecast infrastructure:** KMD produces MAM/OND seasonal outlooks used by health planners

### Health System Structure

```
National Level
  |-- Ministry of Health (policy, standards, coordination)
  |
County Level (47 counties)
  |-- County Health Management Team (CHMT)
  |-- County Director of Health
  |-- County RMNCAH coordinator
  |
Sub-County Level
  |-- Sub-County Health Management Team
  |-- Sub-County Medical Officer of Health
  |
Facility Level
  |-- Level 4-6 hospitals
  |-- Level 2-3 health centers and dispensaries
  |
Community Level
  |-- Community Health Promoters (CHPs) / Community Health Volunteers (CHVs)
  |-- ~100,000 CHVs nationally
  |-- Each covers ~500 households
```

**CHART's entry point:** County RMNCAH coordinator and CHMT. They make seasonal planning decisions and allocate resources to sub-counties.

### MNCH Indicators (Baseline)

- Maternal mortality ratio: ~362 per 100,000 live births (2020)
- Neonatal mortality rate: ~21 per 1,000 live births
- Under-5 mortality rate: ~41 per 1,000 live births
- ANC 4+ visits coverage: ~58%
- Skilled birth attendance: ~70%
- IPTp-3 coverage (malaria prevention in pregnancy): ~40%

### Climate-Health Risks

| Hazard | Affected Regions | Season | MNCH Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| Heat waves | Northeastern, Coastal, Rift Valley | Jan-Mar, Oct | Preterm birth, neonatal dehydration |
| Drought | ASALs (arid/semi-arid lands), 80% of Kenya | Below-normal MAM/OND | Malnutrition, food insecurity |
| Flooding | Lake Victoria basin, Coastal, Tana River | MAM (Mar-May), OND (Oct-Dec) | Waterborne disease, facility access |
| Malaria | Lake Victoria basin, Coastal | Rainy seasons | Malaria in pregnancy, anemia |
| Cholera | Urban informal settlements, flood-prone | Rainy seasons | Child diarrheal mortality |

### Policy Framework

**Kenya RMNCAH-N Investment Framework 2025/26-2029/30:**
- Explicitly integrates climate change interventions alongside MNCH ([Kenya RMNCAH-N](https://countdown2030.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/RMNXAH-N-for-Official-Processing.pdf))
- Projects saving 27,995 child lives, 4,611 maternal lives, preventing 11,071 stillbirths over 5 years
- ROI: KSh 12.50 for every shilling invested
- Implementation matrices include climate adaptation activities

**Kenya Climate Change Act (2016):**
- Established National Climate Change Council
- Required County Climate Change Action Plans
- Health identified as a priority sector

**Kenya National Adaptation Plan (2015-2030):**
- Health sector adaptation priorities include disease surveillance, early warning, community resilience
- MNCH not specifically called out (gap that CHART can help address)

### Digital Infrastructure

- **KHIS (DHIS2):** Operational since 2011, all 47 counties, monthly reporting
- **Mobile penetration:** ~95% (M-Pesa dominant), enabling mHealth interventions
- **Internet connectivity:** Growing but variable; urban areas well-connected, rural gaps remain
- **CHV digital tools:** Some counties have CHV reporting apps (e.g., Medic's Community Health Toolkit)

### Recommended Pilot Counties

| County | Rationale |
|---|---|
| **Kilifi** | CHAMNHA intervention evidence available; coastal heat risk; Aga Khan University presence |
| **Kisumu** | Lake Victoria basin; malaria + flooding; active health research community |
| **Turkana** | Extreme heat + drought; ASAL context; high MNCH burden |
| **Nairobi (informal settlements)** | Urban heat + flooding + cholera; high population density |

## 2. India

### Why India Second

1. **Scale challenge:** 700+ districts across diverse agroecological zones
2. **No national climate-MNCH framework** yet (unlike Kenya's RMNCAH-N)
3. **Emerging state-level efforts:** Rajasthan (Khushi Baby), Ahmedabad (HAP), but not coordinated
4. **Complex governance:** Health is a state subject; central guidelines, state implementation
5. **Strong digital infrastructure:** HMIS on DHIS2, Aadhaar, ASHA worker network
6. **IMD seasonal forecasts** are district-level and well-established

### Health System Structure

```
National Level
  |-- Ministry of Health and Family Welfare (MoHFW)
  |-- National Health Mission (NHM)
  |
State Level (28 states + 8 UTs)
  |-- State Health Department
  |-- State RMNCH+A coordinator
  |
District Level (766 districts)
  |-- District Health Officer / Chief Medical Officer
  |-- District RMNCH+A nodal officer
  |
Block Level (sub-district)
  |-- Block Medical Officer
  |-- Block health team
  |
Facility Level
  |-- District hospitals, CHCs, PHCs, Sub-centers
  |
Community Level
  |-- ASHA workers (~1 million nationally)
  |-- Each covers ~1,000 population
  |-- Anganwadi workers (nutrition/childcare)
```

**CHART's entry point:** District RMNCH+A nodal officer (similar role to Kenya's county RMNCAH coordinator).

### MNCH Indicators (Baseline)

- Maternal mortality ratio: ~97 per 100,000 live births (2020, national; varies widely by state)
- Neonatal mortality rate: ~20 per 1,000 live births (national average)
- Under-5 mortality rate: ~32 per 1,000 live births
- ANC 4+ visits coverage: ~58% (national; <30% in some states)
- Institutional delivery: ~89% (national; <60% in some states)
- Major geographic disparities: UP, Bihar, MP, Rajasthan have highest burden

### Climate-Health Risks

| Hazard | Affected Regions | Season | MNCH Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| Extreme heat | Northwest, Central, Southeast | Apr-Jun | Preterm birth, stillbirth, dehydration |
| Heat waves | Rajasthan, Gujarat, AP, Telangana, UP, Bihar | Mar-Jun | Maternal heat stress, reduced facility visits |
| Flooding | Bihar, Assam, Kerala, Mumbai, Chennai | Jun-Sep (monsoon) | Waterborne disease, displacement, facility access |
| Drought | Maharashtra, Karnataka, Rajasthan, MP | Failed monsoon years | Malnutrition, food insecurity |
| Cyclones | Eastern coast (Odisha, AP, WB) | Oct-Dec | Displacement, infrastructure damage |
| Air pollution | Indo-Gangetic Plain, Delhi NCR | Oct-Feb (winter) | Low birth weight, preterm birth, respiratory illness |

### Existing Climate-Health Initiatives

**Ahmedabad Heat Action Plan (2013):**
- India's first city-level HAP, now a model for 23+ Indian cities ([Exemplars in Global Health](https://www.exemplars.health/stories/ahmedabad-indias-heat-action-plan))
- Components: early warning, public awareness, medical preparedness, cool roofs
- Evaluation showed reduced heat-related mortality (rate ratio 2.34 to 1.25 at 47C) ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6236972/))
- **Gap:** No maternal-specific outcome tracking

**Khushi Baby CHVI (Rajasthan):**
- Village-level Climate Health Vulnerability Index covering 80M people ([Khushi Baby](https://www.khushibaby.org/cause/climate-health))
- PCA + regression on exposure, sensitivity, adaptive capacity ([data.org](https://data.org/playbooks/lessons-from-indias-data-capacity-accelerator-for-climate-and-health/c/fellowships-in-action/creating-a-granular-chvi-and-establishing-causal-links-between-climate-and-health-outcomes/))
- Integrates CHW-collected digital health records
- Establishing causal links between climate and health outcomes
- Supported by India Health Fund + Google

**National Action Plan on Climate Change and Human Health (NAPCCHH):**
- Published by MoHFW
- 4 priority areas: heat stress, air pollution, vector-borne disease, extreme weather
- State-level implementation plans required but progress varies

### Digital Infrastructure

- **HMIS (DHIS2):** Covers all states and districts, monthly reporting from sub-center level
- **Aadhaar:** Unique ID system enabling individual tracking (where permitted)
- **ASHA worker network:** ~1 million ASHAs with growing digital tool adoption
- **RCH Portal:** Reproductive and Child Health tracking system (individual-level)
- **IDSP (DHIS2):** Integrated Disease Surveillance Programme for outbreak detection
- **Mobile penetration:** ~80%, rapidly growing data connectivity

### Recommended Pilot States/Districts

| Location | Rationale |
|---|---|
| **Rajasthan (select districts)** | Khushi Baby CHVI data available; extreme heat; high MNCH burden; ASHA network |
| **Ahmedabad, Gujarat** | HAP already operational; infrastructure for heat response; evaluation data exists |
| **Bihar (flood-prone districts)** | Monsoon flooding; highest MNCH burden; displacement impacts |
| **Odisha (coastal districts)** | Cyclone + heat risk; strong state health system; prior disaster response experience |

## 3. Cross-Country Comparison

| Dimension | Kenya | India |
|---|---|---|
| Administrative units | 47 counties | 766 districts |
| Health workers (community) | ~100,000 CHVs | ~1,000,000 ASHAs |
| DHIS2 maturity | High | High |
| Climate-MNCH policy | Explicit (RMNCAH-N) | Emerging (NAPCCHH) |
| Seasonal calendar | MAM + OND rains | Southwest + Northeast monsoon |
| Primary climate risks | Heat, drought, malaria, flooding | Heat, flooding, air pollution, drought |
| Existing climate-health tools | DHIS2 Climate (pilot) | Khushi Baby CHVI, Ahmedabad HAP |
| Research evidence | CHAMNHA (Kilifi) | Scattered (Rajasthan, Ahmedabad) |
| Implementation complexity | Moderate | High (scale + governance) |
